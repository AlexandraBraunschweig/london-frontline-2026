## Why

There is currently no way to test the hypothesis that shared rides reduce road congestion during a mass evacuation, because there is no population to simulate against. This change produces a synthetic population for Thanet District Council (LAD `E07000114`) — homes, households, family relationships, and cars — together with a seat-allocation plan that gives every person a specific car to walk to (or be collected by) and a seat out. That output is what a later traffic microsimulation (e.g. UDST's MANTA) would consume to compare a one-car-per-household baseline against a ride-shared scenario. Running that comparison is explicitly out of scope for this change.

## What Changes

- New pipeline generating a synthetic population for a given UK Local Authority District from ONS Census 2021 Output-Area marginals (age, sex, license_type, household car availability), matched to OA-level totals. Each person also carries phone_number, name, mobility_status, and medical_skill for coordination purposes.
- New normalized `person_relationships` edge table (not a list-valued field) driving both the dependency graph and indivisible "travel groups": under-10s must travel with both co-resident parents, 10-15s must travel with at least one, and 16+ persons are not mandatorily bound to their household's travel group and may instead join another family's group during assignment. Co-resident parenthood — which marginal-only synthesis does not produce — is derived by an explicit oldest-qualifying-adults rule.
- New home-location assignment placing each synthetic household at a real dwelling point within its Output Area, sourced entirely from the ONS National Statistics UPRN Lookup (NSUL), which carries the UPRN, its coordinates, and its Output Area allocation in one file. Homes are identified by UPRN and coordinates; no human-readable address is produced. Deliberately excludes any council-portal scraping (dropped for portability and ToS reasons; see Impact).
- New vehicle generation: one vehicle per car owned by a household, with a vehicle_type (car for this iteration) and license_plate for identification. Seat capacity is hardcoded (e.g. 5) for this iteration; a realistic capacity distribution is deferred.
- **Muster points are parked cars, not sites.** Each vehicle is parked at the nearest drivable road point within 20 m of its owner household's home, and that parking location *is* a muster point, with capacity equal to that one vehicle's seats. No POI catalog of schools/community centres/car parks is derived or curated.
- New single-pass seat assignment replacing what was previously a separate muster-point assignment and vehicle-packing stage: because one muster point holds exactly one vehicle, choosing a person's muster point and choosing their seat are the same decision. Tiers, in order: the owner household in its own car, then walk-in groups containing a dependent, then home-collection groups, then independent walk-in adults. Walk-in groups are subject to a 10-minute pedestrian-network ceiling; home-collection groups are collected from home and exempt. Every departing vehicle must retain at least one occupant licensed to drive. Anyone who doesn't fit is recorded as unmet demand.
- New `leader-route-planning` output, now a pure rendering step over decisions already made: for each vehicle, a chosen leader (preferring the owner household), an ordered list of stops (its muster point, then any home-collection home locations, by UPRN and coordinates) each with a meeting time and the list of people expected there (name, phone), and a single fleet-wide evacuation destination. Includes the notification fallback rule: a person with no phone has their `dependent_of` carer notified instead; a person with neither is marked for physical collection.
- Schema designed to be internationally portable (a generic `AdminArea{level, parent_id}` hierarchy rather than hardcoded UK OA/LSOA/MSOA fields), even though every data source used in this iteration is UK-specific.
- Explicitly deferred to future iterations (not built now): full bus modeling (a bus's route start point differs from any owner's household), in-app route/pickup confirmation mechanisms, multiple or capacity-constrained destinations, and a trust-network model for who someone would prefer to ride with.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities
- `synthetic-population`: generation of synthetic person and household records (age, sex, license_type, phone_number, name, mobility_status, medical_skill) matched to OA-level Census marginals, plus co-resident parent derivation, the `person_relationships` edge table, and the age-banded dependency rule that groups household members into indivisible travel groups.
- `home-location-assignment`: placement of each synthetic household at a real dwelling point within its Output Area from NSUL, identified by UPRN and coordinates.
- `vehicle-and-muster-points`: synthesis of one vehicle per household car with a fixed seat capacity, vehicle_type, and license_plate; parking each vehicle at the nearest drivable road point within a configured snap distance of its owner's home; and the definition of a muster point as exactly that one parked vehicle.
- `evacuation-seat-assignment`: the collection-mode split (home collection vs. walk-in) and the single tiered allocation of people into specific vehicles' seats, enforcing the walking ceiling, keeping families together, spreading collection stops, enforcing driver availability, and recording unmet demand.
- `leader-route-planning`: per-vehicle leader selection and route rendering (stops, meeting times, people lists, destination) and the phone/carer notification-fallback rule.

### Modified Capabilities
None — this is a greenfield repository with no existing specs.

## Impact

- New external data dependencies: ONS Census 2021 (OA-level tables via NOMIS), ONS geography boundaries API, ONS National Statistics UPRN Lookup, OpenStreetMap (pedestrian network and drivable road centrelines), `osmnet`/`pandana` for the pedestrian network used in walking-time queries.
- No dwelling dataset beyond NSUL is used. OS Open UPRN was dropped once NSUL was found to carry the same coordinates alongside the Output Area allocation, making it a 618 MB download for nothing. OS Open USRN was dropped with the address requirement. Overture Buildings and OSM address points were dropped earlier as a fallback for a case that does not arise.
- No routed driving graph is built. Road data is used only as line geometry, to snap each vehicle to a parking point; home-collection proximity and stop timings use straight-line distance. Network-routed driving is the downstream microsimulation's concern.
- Reuses the Dagster + DuckDB (spatial extension) pipeline pattern from the `northcray/data` reference project, but deliberately drops its Bexley-council-portal Playwright address scrape — that step is fragile, ToS-grey-area, and hard-coupled to one council's site, which conflicts with this change's goal of working for any LAD.
- Does not include the downstream MANTA traffic microsimulation (baseline-vs-pooled congestion comparison) — that is a separate, later change consuming this one's output.
- No existing code or specs are affected; this is the first change in the repository.
